v1.0.61 2026-07-16

180db Changelog — July 16, 2026

New: Snapshots

  • Store any window into a slot with ⌥⌘1–9 and recall it with ⌘1–9 (also File ▸ Store/Recall Snapshot).
  • A snapshot is a frozen copy of the whole window — playlist, layout, volume, pinned state — and survives closing the window and relaunching the app.
  • Recalling opens a fresh window each time. Edits to a recalled window don't change the stored snapshot; store again to update a slot.

New: Playlist Search

  • Press ⌘F and type a few letters — fuzzy matching finds tracks from fragments ("boc mus" finds "Boards of Canada – Music Is Math").
  • The top hit is always highlighted: Enter plays it, ⇧Enter jumps to it in the playlist without playing.
  • Closing the search leaves your playlist scroll and selection exactly where they were.
  • The status bar shows how many tracks match; right-click a result for Reveal in Finder / Skip / Remove.

Fixes

  • WAV files from sample packs that previously failed to open now load and show waveforms.
  • Seeking with the digit keys (0–9) on short tracks no longer jumps to the next track by itself.
  • On macOS 14/15, the playhead, timecode, and album cover no longer freeze until you move the mouse.
  • Cover-only mode now handles non-square artwork: the window follows the cover's shape instead of forcing a square.

Playback

  • With shuffle on, Next/Previous now scrolls the playlist to the new track — from the keyboard, the on-screen buttons, and media keys alike.
  • New cycle (repeat) control in the status bar; cycle mode is remembered per window.

Menus & Shortcuts

  • Display toggles now use checkmarks ("✓ Artist Name") instead of Show/Hide wording — in the View menu and all right-click menus.
  • Copy "Artist – Title" from a track's right-click menu, or press ⌘C on selected rows. The status bar confirms what was copied.
  • "Search with Google" on any track's right-click menu.
  • Reveal in Finder is now ⌥R and works app-wide. ⌘R now copies the 30-second buffer; Clear Buffer is ⇧⌘R.

Status Bar

  • The total playlist duration is now clickable — click to toggle exact duration.
  • Shuffle and cycle icons are easier to hit.

Performance

  • Many engine and rendering improvements under the hood for smoother playback and lower idle load.
v1.0.36 2026-06-30

June 30 - 180db v1.0.35

Features

  • Cue — CDJ-style cueing. Drop a cue point, preview from it, and launch playback from exactly that spot.

  • C sets and triggers the cue. Hold C to preview from the cue point; release to snap back to it.

  • With C held, Space launches playback from the cue point.

  • X clears the cue.

  • , and . (< > buttons) nudge the cue point backward and forward in fine steps. If no cue is set yet, the first nudge drops one at the playhead.

  • Shift-, and Shift-. make larger jumps.

  • = flash-zooms the waveform to the cue, or to the playhead when there's no cue. Pressing again will get back to the normal view

  • Transient mode — Turn on Audio > Transient Detection and 180db analyzes the track's transients. With it on, Shift-, and Shift-. snap the cue to the previous and next transient instead of nudging, so you can place cues precisely on the hit.

  • Album covers in the playlist — Turn on View > Show Cover Image to show album art in your playlist rows, with a placeholder for tracks that have no artwork.

Quality of Life

  • Faster scrolling and loading on large playlists. Cover thumbnails and waveforms are now cached and reused, and refresh automatically when a file changes on disk.

Bug Fixes

  • Fixed audio clicks and pops around cue points and track changes.

v1.0.88 2026-08-11

180db Changelog — August 11, 2026

New: Multi-Window Snapshots

  • ⌥⌘1–9 now stores all open windows as one snapshot, not just the active one. ⌘1–9 recalls the whole group at once — every window comes back exactly where it was, with its playlist, layout, volume, and playback position.
  • Recalling a snapshot replaces your current workspace — and that's undoable: ⌘Z closes the recalled windows and brings your previous ones back exactly as they were.
  • The cue point and mute state now survive closing a window, quitting the app, and snapshots.
  • Storing into an occupied slot is now undoable too — ⌘Z brings the old snapshot back, ⇧⌘Z re-applies the new one. Overwriting a slot no longer silently destroys what was there.

New: Undo

  • ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z now work across the app: playlist edits (adding, removing, moving tracks), closing a window, opening a new window, duplicating a window, recalling a snapshot, removing a snapshot, and overwriting a snapshot slot.
  • Closed a window by accident? ⌘Z reopens it with its playlist, position, and layout intact.
  • Every undo and redo announces what it did in the status bar.
  • Undo also works while the ⌘P palette is open — remove a snapshot there, ⌘Z reinstates it without leaving the palette.

New: Recents (⌘P)

  • Press ⌘P for a Spotlight-style palette of everything you've ever opened — newest first, grouped by day. Type a few letters to fuzzy-search it.
  • Enter opens the highlighted entry in a new window and starts playing; ⌘Enter (or ⌘click) adds it to the window you were in without interrupting playback.
  • Your stored snapshots float above the palette as cover-art cards — click one to recall it.
  • Items dropped or opened together show as one entry ("Name, +3 items") and re-open together.
  • Opening a single track from Finder now imports its whole folder and starts on the track you clicked — and re-opening it from Recents does the same.
  • Backspace with an empty search field removes the highlighted entry; right-click for Open in New Window / Add to Active Window / Show in Finder / Store Snapshot / Remove.
  • File ▸ Open Recent shows the newest entries as a regular menu; File ▸ Export Recents… saves the full list as CSV.

Fixes

  • Fixed three long-standing multi-window staleness bugs: the album cover sticking on a previous track, the ▶ play indicator freezing in the playlist, and album names only appearing after clicking a window.
  • Fixed a serious bug where a drag that left the window could break drag & drop system-wide (all apps, Finder included) until 180db quit.
  • Seeking no longer pops — jumps splice smoothly instead of clicking.
  • Tracks no longer start with their first moment silently cut off after switching from another track.
  • Snapshot slots can no longer turn into "zombies" (a stored slot that showed a card but recalled nothing). Storing is now crash-safe, and the app repairs any previously damaged slots at launch.
  • ⌘Z is refused while files are importing — it could previously resurrect old windows mid-import.
  • ⌘N no longer copies a stale window size from a window that had been resized by dragging its edges.
  • Rapidly toggling the playlist (⌥⌘P) no longer wedges the window into walking down the screen.
  • Toggling window elements no longer shows the old layout for a beat before catching up.
  • Reordering tracks by drag now lands on the exact rows you picked up, even if the playlist changed under the drag.

Performance

  • Track switching is much faster, and pressing Next plays the already-prepared next track instantly (gapless).
  • ⌘P opens instantly — snapshot cards are prepared when you store, not when you open the palette.
  • Windows open faster and playlist redraws are smoother: large parts of the player window were rebuilt on native AppKit views.

Menus & Shortcuts

  • Show in Finder is now ⇧⌘R and lives in the File menu and every track context menu.
